DISPLAY / FIRST LIGHT

The screen speaks

A 0.69-inch, 96 × 16 pixel strip — the pen's entire voice. First it had to say anything at all; then it had to say it in Ukrainian.

03 FIRST BOOT

The clone SSD1306 controller came alive and the firmware's greeting — "PENNY hello :)" — appeared on a strip thinner than a fingernail. Everything the pen ever says to its owner passes through it.

DISPLAY / SURGERY

Cutting the donor board

The panel ships on a development PCB far longer than the pen is wide. The driver circuitry had to stay. The rest met the cutting disc.

05 THE CUT

The board couldn't simply be cut — capacitors and resistors lived in the cut zone. The right guy, found by wandering the radio market, moved them to new spots first; then the dead half came off…

THE GLITCH

…everything worked for about a day — until the display began dimming by itself, waking to full brightness only under a finger on its top-right corner. A cracked joint from the cut: flux, iron, rework — fixed.

DISPLAY / SETBACK

Torn off, restacked

The display that survived surgery didn't survive assembly — and its replacement accidentally improved the design.

07 THE TEAR

During an enclosure test-fit the panel separated from its flex cable. The electronics survived; the glass and its ribbon did not. Two spares were waiting in the drawer — one stepped in.

09

PYTHON CAD

Find the mistakes before the printer does

ONE SOURCE OF TRUTH

The entire pen is one Python file — every dimension, every part, every wire, every check.

Change one number and the script rebuilds all the parts and re-runs every physical test.
01 / WIRES

Wires take up space — and you normally find that out during assembly.

The script models every wire as a solid object and checks it against the shell and the boards. A route that doesn't fit fails on screen, not on the desk.

02 / ASSEMBLY

A part can fit inside the pen and still be impossible to put in.

The script slides each part along its entry path. If the battery, board, camera or display can't make it in, the build stops before anything prints.

03 / MOVEMENT

The clip has to swing all the way around without scraping the cap.

The script rotates the clip in 45° steps and checks for collisions at every stop — 90°, where the cap is widest, is the hard one.

04 / PRINTING

Exporting parts and arranging the print plate by hand invites mistakes.

The script exports all three bodies and lays out the print plate itself — the same way, every time.

05 / FEEDBACK

Some fixes are faster to make by hand in a CAD app — but the hand edit has to get back into the script.

Export the part before and after the hand fix. The script compares the two shapes and turns the difference into exact numbers for the master file.

CAD / HAND FIXES

Pointing at geometry is hard

Some fixes are faster to make by hand in Shapr3D. The hard part was telling the AI where the hand had been.

ARROWS AND CROSSES

Marked-up screenshots looked like the obvious way to point at a bad wall or a phantom notch — arrows, crosses, red boxes. The agent agreed every time, then edited somewhere else. Pixels don't map to solids.

DIFF INSTEAD OF DESCRIBING

What worked: make the fix by hand in Shapr3D, export the body before and after, and let the model diff the two solids. The diff named the exact faces every time — and the fix went back into the Python master.

DETAILS

Three small wars

None of these was in the plan. Each one is why the pen works outside a demo.

17 THE DISPLAY REFUSED TO DIM

The OLED clone ignores every software dimming path — contrast writes, register pokes, dithering. The fix is physical: a tint film over the window. Boot UX shrank to three glyphs: ... booting, + online, ! no WiFi.

ONE ROTATION, THREE CODEBASES

The sensor has no portrait mode. Rotate the view and the whole stack follows: the live stream turns in the browser, captures get their pixels rotated on the server, and the pen's display flips to stay readable.

18 POCKET INFRASTRUCTURE

iOS kills a locked phone's hotspot in ~20 seconds — fatal for a pen that lives in a pocket. A custom TrollStore app keeps Personal Hotspot advertising with the app closed and the phone locked, and the hotspot hides under a boring printer SSID.

REAL-WORLD TEST

Autofocus was not good enough

The complete system worked, but the final real-world capture exposed the remaining problem: autofocus did not produce consistently readable screen images. It passed integration, not the reality check.

SYSTEM WORKEDIMAGE QUALITY DID NOT

The project's first feasibility note — written before any hardware existed — named optics and focus as the top risk. The last test agrees: the risk register was right all along.
